Why Unidle

Small on purpose.

One window, one button and a tray icon. Everything else is about staying out of your way.

One button

Click to turn it on, click again to turn it off. From the window, the tray menu or a global shortcut.

Keeps you Available

Asks the OS not to sleep, and nudges the pointer by one pixel and back so chat apps see activity. It skips the nudge while you work, and never clicks or types characters.

Safe to forget

Turning it off, quitting or even a crash gives your computer back its normal sleep settings.

Private and light

No network access, no telemetry, no account. It lives in your tray with a tiny footprint.

Timers and schedule

Runs on its own when you want it to.

Set it for a while, or for your working hours every week, and stop thinking about it.

  • Quick timers. Keep awake for 15 minutes, 30 minutes, an hour, two hours or until a time, from the tray or the window. When it ends, Unidle turns off.
  • A weekly schedule. Pick the days and hours, as many times a day as you like, overnight included, plus days off for holidays.
  • Always says what's next. The tray and window show why it's on and when that changes. Turn it off by hand and the schedule pauses until that time ends.
  • Follows your clock. Daylight saving changes are handled, and after sleep it catches up within seconds. Export the schedule to back it up or use it on another computer.

Limitations

Know before you install.

Unidle is honest about what it does and doesn't do. A few things to know first.

On a work computer, assume it can be seen

Managed work and school machines usually run device management, security and activity-tracking software. IT can typically see the apps you install, the processes you run and simulated input. Unidle doesn't hide from any of this and never will.

Check your organization's IT policy before installing, or ask your IT team. If you only need the screen to stay on for a presentation or a long build, turn off Simulate input in Settings: Unidle then only asks the OS not to sleep. Using it to look active when you aren't working can break your employer's rules, and people have lost their jobs over it.

Wayland on Linux

Wayland doesn't let apps move the pointer, so Unidle keeps the system awake without the nudge. Chat apps may still show you as Away.

macOS permission

The pointer nudge needs the Accessibility permission. Without it, Unidle still keeps your Mac awake.

Schedules need the app running

The schedule can only turn Unidle on while it's running. Turn on Launch at login so it's always there.

Not code-signed yet

Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper ask you to confirm the first launch, and some security tools flag unsigned apps.
