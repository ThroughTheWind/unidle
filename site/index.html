Why Unidle

Small on purpose.

One window, one button, a tray icon. Everything else is about staying out of your way.

One button, that's it

Click to turn it on, click again to turn it off. Use the window, the tray menu or a keyboard shortcut.

Stays awake two ways

Asks the OS not to sleep, and nudges the pointer by one pixel and straight back, so apps that watch for input see activity too.

Never gets in your way

Skips its nudge while you are working. It never clicks, types characters or changes the focused window.

Safe to forget

Turning it off, quitting or even a crash gives your computer back its normal sleep settings.

Private by design

No network access, no telemetry, no account. The code is small enough to read in an afternoon.

Lives in your tray

Close the window and it keeps running quietly in the tray or menu bar, with a tiny footprint.

Free, for every platform, from GitHub Releases.

Your system
Download installer (.exe) or the .msi package

macOS

macOS 12 or later, Apple silicon and Intel

Your system
Download disk image (.dmg)

The pointer nudge needs the Accessibility permission. Without it, Unidle still keeps your Mac awake.

Linux

x86-64, X11 and Wayland

Your system
Download AppImage or the .deb package

On Wayland, Unidle keeps the system awake without the pointer nudge.

Builds are not code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper ask you to confirm the first launch. You can also build it from source or browse all releases.

Open source, MIT licensed.
